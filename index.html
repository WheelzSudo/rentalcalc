Rental Property Investment Metrics & Benchmarks

Accurate cash flow modeling prevents real estate investors from underestimating property holding costs. Overlooking unallocated capital expenditures (CapEx) or localized vacancy fluctuations can convert an apparently profitable rental into a cash-negative asset.

Metric Name Target Investor Benchmark Primary Calculation Focus
Net Monthly Cash Flow $200 - $400+ per door Net revenue after mortgage, taxes, insurance, & reserves
Operating Expense Ratio (OER) 35% - 45% of rental income Non-mortgage property maintenance & operating overhead
Maintenance & CapEx Reserve 8% - 12% of gross rent Roofing, HVAC replacement, plumbing, and wear-and-tear repairs
Vacancy Reserve Rate 5% - 8% (approx. 2-4 weeks/yr) Holding expenses during tenant turnover periods

How to Calculate Rental Cash Flow Formula

To calculate true rental property net cash flow, subtract operating expenses and debt service from gross revenue:

Monthly Operating Expenses = (Property Taxes ÷ 12) + (Insurance ÷ 12) + Maintenance Reserve + Vacancy Reserve

Net Monthly Cash Flow = Monthly Rent - (Mortgage Payment + Monthly Operating Expenses)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good cash flow for a rental property?

A standard target for residential single-family homes or multi-family units is $200 to $400 in net monthly cash flow per door after accounting for all debt obligations, taxes, insurance, and maintenance reserves.

What is the 50% rule in real estate investing?

The 50% rule is a quick screening guideline estimating that approximately 50% of gross rental income will go toward operating expenses (excluding mortgage principal and interest).

What is the difference between Cap Rate and Cash-on-Cash Return?

Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate) evaluates a property's unleveraged yield based on Net Operating Income (NOI) divided by purchase price. Cash-on-Cash Return calculates leveraged yield based on annual net cash flow divided by total out-of-pocket cash invested (down payment, closing costs, and rehab).
